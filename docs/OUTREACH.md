# Resume, GitHub Profile and Emailing Professors (Funded Master's Applications)

## 0. Be realistic about what this project does for you
One well-done project shows you can run a data science workflow properly. It will not by itself win funding. Funded research master's offers depend mostly on **fit with a specific professor's work**, grades, and evidence of research potential (a thesis, paper, internship, or a project that goes beyond a tutorial). So: do this project well, then add depth (see NOTES.md Part 6), and aim to have **2-3 solid projects** plus one that connects to a professor's area.

### 1.2 Resume structure for a research master's application (1 page)
1. Header: name, email, GitHub, LinkedIn, (Google Scholar if any)
2. Education: degree, university, CGPA/percentage, relevant coursework (ML, statistics, linear algebra, DSA)
3. Research / Projects: 2-3 entries as above
4. Experience / internships (if any)
5. Skills: Python, PyTorch/TensorFlow (only if used), scikit-learn, pandas, SQL, Git, LaTeX
6. Publications / achievements / certifications (only meaningful ones)

Build it in **Overleaf** using a clean free template (search "Overleaf resume template"), or Google Docs. Export as PDF named `FirstName_LastName_CV.pdf`.

### 1.3 Statement of purpose (you will need it)
Structure: (1) what question excites you, (2) the work that prepared you (this project plus others), (3) why this program and these faculty, (4) what you want to do next. Make it specific to each program.

## 3. Finding programs and funding

**Search by research area, not just university rankings.**
1. Decide 2-3 topics (examples: climate/weather ML, time-series forecasting, applied ML for earth science)
2. Look up recent papers on Google Scholar, arXiv, Semantic Scholar; note the authors and their universities
3. Read the lab pages and "Prospective students" pages
4. Build a spreadsheet: professor, university, topic, recent paper, funding info, deadline, status, date emailed

**Funding routes (general picture; verify details on each official page, because rules and deadlines change):**
- **Thesis-based (research) master's** are where funding usually comes from, via a supervisor's grant (research assistantship) or scholarships. Common in Canada and parts of Europe. Course-based master's are rarely funded.
- **US:** funded master's are uncommon; funding usually appears for PhDs. Some research MS programs offer TA/RA positions.
- **Germany:** public universities charge little or no tuition; look at DAAD scholarships and research assistant (HiWi) jobs
- **Other European and Nordic countries:** national scholarship schemes and university scholarships exist; check each country's official portal
- **Australia / others:** research degrees often come with scholarships; check university pages
- **Home-country and external scholarships:** check government and foundation schemes available to Indian students

**Always confirm on the official university and scholarship websites**; I have not verified current deadlines or amounts for you.

## 4. Emailing professors

### 4.1 Rules that matter
- Write to professors whose work you have actually read; mention one specific paper
- Short: 150-200 words
- One email per professor; no mass BCC
- Attach a 1-page CV (PDF) and link your GitHub
- Subject line should be specific
- Ask a concrete question (are you taking students for [term], is there funding)
- Follow up once after 7-10 days; then move on
- Check the lab page first, because some professors want applicants to apply formally and do not answer cold emails
- Many will not reply. That is normal, so contact 20-40 well-chosen people over time

### 4.2 Template A: cold email
**Subject:** Prospective MS student, interest in [specific topic] / [Your Name]

Dear Professor [Last Name],

I am [Your Name], a recent AI/ML graduate from [University], India. I am applying for a research master's starting [term/year] and am interested in working on [specific topic].

I read your paper "[Paper title]" ([year]) and was particularly interested in [one specific, accurate point about the method or finding]. It connects to a project I recently completed, where I built a forecasting pipeline for next-day rainfall and temperature using 11 years of Hyderabad weather data, compared against a persistence baseline with a chronological split ([GitHub link]).

Are you currently accepting master's students for [term], and is funding (assistantship or scholarship) available through your group? I have attached my CV and would be glad to share more detail about my project.

Thank you for your time and consideration.

Sincerely,
[Name]
[University, degree, CGPA]
[GitHub] | [LinkedIn]

### 4.3 Template B: follow-up (after 7-10 days)
**Subject:** Re: Prospective MS student, interest in [specific topic]

Dear Professor [Last Name],

I wanted to follow up on my earlier message in case it was missed. I remain very interested in your work on [topic] and would appreciate any guidance on whether your group takes master's students for [term].

Thank you,
[Name]

### 4.4 Template C: after they reply positively
- Thank them promptly
- Answer exactly what they asked
- Ask about the formal application process and deadline
- Offer to share a short project summary or have a call
- Do not oversell or promise results you do not have

### 4.5 Mistakes to avoid
- "Dear Sir/Madam" or the wrong name
- Praising their work without specifics
- Claiming your project "predicts weather accurately" (say exactly what it does and how it compares to a baseline)
- Long paragraphs about yourself
- Asking "do you have funding?" with no sign you read their work
- Sending the same email with only the name swapped
